# Certification readiness

> How this platform prepares PaketPort Systems GmbH for an ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification audit — what the auditor receives, how it maps to what they ask for, and an honest account of what is ready versus what a real certification push would still need.

ISO/IEC 27001 certification is granted by an accredited certification body after a **two-stage audit**. This platform's job is to make both stages fast and evidence-led by turning the register into an auditor-ready **audit pack** on demand. For the underlying method see [methodology](./methodology.md); for unfamiliar terms see the [glossary](./glossary.md).

## The two audit stages

**Stage 1 — documentation and readiness review.** The auditor checks that the ISMS *exists on paper* and is ready to be tested: is the scope defined, is there a security policy, a risk assessment and treatment plan, and a Statement of Applicability? Is management engaged? Stage 1 identifies gaps before the deeper audit, so the organisation can fix them. This platform answers Stage 1 almost entirely from documents: `data/organization.yaml` (scope, boundary, policy), the SoA, the risk register and the executive summary.

**Stage 2 — implementation audit.** The auditor tests whether the controls the SoA claims are applicable are **actually operating**, by sampling evidence and interviewing owners. Here the evidence index — with its hashes, collectors and validity windows — and the per-control status and review dates do the heavy lifting.

## What the auditor receives from the audit pack

The audit pack is a byte-reproducible bundle (Markdown plus PDF, built into `dist/`) whose sections follow the order an ISO 27001 auditor actually works through:

| Audit-pack section | What the certification auditor is asking | How it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Executive summary** | "Show me scope, posture and management commitment." | One page: scope statement, headline KPIs, and the Managing Director's commitment. |
| **Statement of Applicability** | "Which controls apply, and why?" | All 93 Annex A:2022 controls with an applicability decision and inclusion reason. |
| **Control register** | "Are the applicable controls implemented and owned?" | Each documented control with owner, status, maturity, policies and linked evidence. |
| **Risk register** | "Is risk assessed and treated to defined criteria?" | The 5×5 model, inherent vs residual scores, treatment and acceptance decisions. |
| **Evidence index** | "Prove the controls operate — and that the proof is intact." | 18 artifacts, each with a **SHA-256** hash, collection date and validity window. |
| **NIS2 readiness** | "How do you meet your parallel regulatory duties?" | Art. 21(2) measures (and the Art. 20 duty) mapped to the controls that satisfy them (82.6% overall). |
| **Improvement actions** | "Do you find and fix weaknesses?" | The CAPA backlog with owners, sources and due dates. |
| **Audit status & expiry** | "Is your documentation current, or stale?" | The draft → reviewed → accepted → expired state per record, plus overdue reviews. |

Because the KPIs are **computed from the register as of a pinned date** (`evidence/metrics.json`), the pack cannot drift from the underlying data — a property auditors value.

## What's ready vs. what's a gap

This repository is a **representative subset**, sized to demonstrate the method convincingly rather than to certify a real company. It is important to be honest about that line.

**Ready now:**

- A complete SoA covering **all 93** Annex A:2022 controls, so no control is silently ignored.
- **25 controls documented in depth** (26.9%) with owners, evidence, risk links and review dates; the remaining 68 are covered at SoA level.
- **76%** of those documented controls implemented or operating, backed by **18 hash-anchored artifacts**, five of them generated by real Linux evidence collectors.
- A worked risk assessment (**13 risks**, none residual High/Critical, **2** formally accepted) and a live improvement backlog (**5 open actions**).
- An internal-audit report and management-review minutes already in evidence.

**What a real certification push would add:**

- **Full control records for all 93 controls**, not 25 — every applicable control with implementation detail and its own evidence.
- An **external penetration test** of the internet-facing lockers and cloud APIs by an independent tester.
- A **complete internal-audit programme** covering the whole ISMS on a planned schedule, rather than a single sample report.
- A **formal management-review cadence** (typically at least annual) with a documented history of decisions and objective-setting.
- Broader evidence coverage so that every operating control, not a subset, is provable at Stage 2.

## NIS2 as a parallel driver

PaketPort is an **essential entity** under NIS2 (Directive (EU) 2022/2555), so certification is not the only clock. The directive's Art. 21(2) risk-management measures and Art. 23 incident-reporting deadlines (24-hour early warning, 72-hour notification, one-month final report) are tracked in the same register and reported in the NIS2 section of the audit pack. Because NIS2 and ISO 27001 share so much ground, the same controls and evidence serve both — one management system, two regulatory outcomes.
